## Success and rejection criteria

- State one precise nontrivial new theorem, algorithm or counterexample with
  its full assumptions and a reproducible proof/certificate.
- Identify the closest prior statements by theorem, equation and page. Test
  whether the result is already stated, equivalent after translation, or an
  immediate routine corollary. Reject these as novelty candidates.
- Prefer resolving an explicitly published open question or conjecture, with
  follow-up and citation searches for intervening solutions. A negative search
  result, changed notation or merely new software packaging is insufficient.
- An independent reviewer must check proof correctness and a separate
  source-grounded comparison must support the precise contribution claim.
- Scientific priority is assessed against inspected literature; never claim
  exhaustive knowledge of every source or convert uncertainty into novelty.
- If current candidates fail, retain why they failed and select a sourced
  unresolved question. Do not improve presentation as a substitute for results.
