## Actionable interpretation

Do not market relative regret, robust conductance allocation, endpoint ratios, minimizing a maximum of two smooth functions, or a mixed-strategy linear program as new generic methods. The first two have inspected network-design precedents above; the latter are elementary mathematical constructions whose exact connection must be proved, not turned into a priority claim from this search. The proposed family result's potential distinguishing content is its graph-specific positivity/coercivity, exact finite strength candidate reduction and fully certified balancing example. This assessment has NOT shown that combination is original or absent elsewhere.

Before promoting randomization, specify the order: choose a distribution, adversary chooses workload without observing the realized draw, then average loss over the draw. If the workload can respond to the realized action, it is a different minimax problem; a distributional LP cannot silently substitute for that information structure. This is an investigator modeling requirement, not an attribution to either inspected paper. Likewise randomized algorithms used to compute a deterministic network are not randomized intervention policies. No retrieved source resolves that exact timing issue for the present graph family.

The strength proposal's linear balancing equation results from canceling common(m+t)/(1+rt) within one edge's two normalized endpoint objectives. This is candidate algebra, not a theorem found in these sources. Exact endpoint/balancing-strength reduction was not identified in the two inspected fulltext models; randomized-policy equivalence remains an open source question. Bounded non-identification cannot support a novelty certificate, publication clearance or physical-benefit claim.
