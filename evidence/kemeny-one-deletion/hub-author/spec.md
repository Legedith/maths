Freeze: 2026-09-08, after the first idea round. Remain within network edge
design. This is a separate hypothesis, not part of the accepted release.

Let a,b,c>=3, a<=b,c, and G=K_(a,b,c) join K_1. Select two distinct
vertices u,v in the a-part and denote the universal vertex by h. Form
H=G-uh. Target: prove or refute K(H+uv)-K(H)<0 for every admitted tuple.
Use the same unweighted simple random walk, stationary-target convention,
and strict sign. Do not assume this deletion is worst among all types;
the experiments suggesting that dominance do not prove it.

An independent-twin partition into {u},{v},A\{u,v},B,C,{h} has six cells.
Give an explicit proof for any quotient/spectral reduction used. For an
equitable partition whose nontrivial cells are independent twin classes,
the omitted modes have transition eigenvalue zero; the resulting additive
contribution to K cancels between H and H+uv. This suggested reduction is
part of the claim to justify, not an accepted black-box oracle.

Generate at least three possible proof/refutation approaches before selecting
one. At most two bounded symbolic/diagnostic batches; freeze each command and
range first, keep all raw attempts and failures, use exact arithmetic, and
stop an unproductive symbolic route rather than launch unbounded expansion.
Check the proposed quotient against full exact matrices at three small
declared tuples. A universal sign claim needs a full-domain proof/certificate;
finite checks alone do not suffice.
