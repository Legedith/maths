C1 (citation; S1): Hu and Kirkland, Complete multipartite graphs and Braess edges, 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.laa.2019.05.035. Primary repository https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/04a4246d-2b67-4e4c-9c4b-60f0c5417031/content ; publisher https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379519302526 . Locators: abstract; Theorem 3.2.3 PDF p12, reviewed pinned source/domain notes. Formula and multipartite conditions concern the intact complete multipartite graph; general twin-edge update requires equal neighborhoods. In H, deg(u)=b+c whereas deg(v)=b+c+1. Therefore they are not twins, and H is not complete multipartite: u is nonadjacent to both v and h although v and h are adjacent. This is direct candidate-graph reasoning. The accepted intact-graph sign/ranking theorem cannot be silently applied after deletion.

C2 (citation; S2): Breen, deBlieck, Vander Meulen, Kemeny's constant and Braess cliques in graphs, arXiv v1 dated 2026-08-04, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04150v1 . C3 (citation; S3): Altafini, Bini, Cutini, Meini, Poloni, An Edge Centrality Measure Based on the Kemeny Constant, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 44(2), 2023. ## Bounded verdict

C8 (conclusion, supported by C1-C7 and search record): No inspected source states or immediately supplies the candidate's strict full-domain sign conclusion after ordinary hub-edge deletion, nor the proposed every-original-edge-deletion robustness theorem. This is a bounded non-identification, not a novelty certificate or evidence that the result remained open. The candidate is a sign guarantee for one insertion; it is not yet Kemeny-optimal edge selection in the damaged graph. Both quotient compression and generic edge-update formulas are already available. A paper pitched mainly as a new quotient algorithm or new ability to update K under deletion would substantially overlap known work.

## Three falsifiable follow-ons, all proposed rather than established

F1 (methodological proposal; C1,C2,C3): Prove or refute K((G-f)+uv)<K(G-f) for every original edge f and every pair u,v in any original minimum part. Freeze quantifiers before testing. Classify endpoint incidences under part-preserving symmetry, split affected vertices from twin classes, and certify each resulting rational sign. Meaning: an idealized one-link-failure tolerance guarantee for an already selected upgrade. Requires all incidence cases and ties, not a sampled deletion set.

F2 (methodological proposal; C3,C5): Solve the actual post-failure one-edge choice: compare every missing-edge orbit in H, including restoration uh, versus uv and untouched within-part edges. Seek exact optimality regions in a,b,c, rather than assuming the intact size rule survives. Meaning: distinguish repairing a failed link from adding an alternate link under a one-unit intervention budget. Costs, allowed repairs, and the workload objective must be specified; no real deployment benefit is demonstrated.

F3 (methodological proposal; C2,C3,C6): Generalize exact evaluation to bounded defects: given an r-part partition and d changed edges, isolate their endpoints and retain unaffected twin cells; derive complete spectral correction plus quotient update, then state operation count in r,d separately from graph recognition and integer bit cost. Meaning: a reusable evaluator for modestly imperfect ideal architectures. The quotient principle is prior work; any contribution needs a precise parameterized algorithm/guarantee and fair comparison to existing rank-update methods, not an unbenchmarked speedup claim.
