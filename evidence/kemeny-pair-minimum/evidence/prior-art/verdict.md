# Bounded prior-art verdict

No published minimum-order theorem or complete small-graph enumeration for the exact two-nonedge predicate was located in four targeted queries and four inspected primary texts.

The closest exact result remains Faught, Kempton, and Knudson's `P7` example: adding `{1,3}` and `{5,7}` jointly is Braess, while neither singleton is Braess. This proves only `n <= 7`. The other reviewed works count **single** Braess edges in tree families, study **clique** additions of three or more edges, or study a single edge between pendant paths. None reports exclusion of all connected graphs below order seven.

The computation is worth running at the proposed small scale. The sourced `P7` witness reduces the minimum question to a finite exact census below order seven, with a simple useful outcome: either a smaller witness or a complete no-smaller certificate. The method and `P7` phenomenon are already known; the only prospective added fact is the certified minimum order.

This review does not establish that the question is globally open or publication-novel. In particular, the 2023 enumeration paper cites `arXiv:2306.04005`, *Edge addition and the change in Kemeny's constant*, which was outside the four-source cap and should be checked, together with citation links, before any novelty claim. Until then, label a successful result a **candidate exact minimum-order computation**.

No numerical experiment was run. Exact sources, local hashes, locators, query terms, and within-document search limits are recorded in `source-notes.json`.
