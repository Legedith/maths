# Post-result prior-art verdict

No exact prior-art blocker was located for the specific `E?zW` witness or the minimum-order-six result under the frozen predicate

`K(G+e) <= K(G), K(G+f) <= K(G), K(G+e+f) > K(G)`.

That conclusion is bounded to six targeted queries, three newly inspected relevant primary papers, and the previously reviewed sources. It does not establish global novelty.

Several ingredients are clearly established prior art:

- Faught, Kempton, and Knudson already define Braess sets and give the published `P7` two-edge interaction. Their Braess-set definition only requires a strict joint increase; it does not require nonincreasing singleton additions.
- Hu and Kirkland define non-Braess edges using `<= 0`, give exact-zero edge-addition examples, and provide a general formula for adding an edge between nonadjacent twins.
- Candidate edge `e={1,2}` is between twins because both endpoints have neighborhood `{5,6}`. Its single-edge change, and its changed behavior after adding `f`, are therefore instances of an existing formula once the quotient quantities are evaluated.
- Candidate edge `f={3,4}` joins pendant vertices with different neighbors. Ciardo's strict pendant-twin theorem and Kim's common-root twin-pendent-path construction do not apply to it.

The defensible result wording is: by excluding every connected graph of orders two through five and exhibiting an order-six witness, the sealed two-evaluator finite census supports a candidate minimum order of six within the stated graph class and convention, pending its designated final semantic gate. This is a minimum-order statement, not a classification of larger graphs. The located `E?zW` witness is smaller than the reviewed published `P7` example. Claims of a first-known example, a new Braess-set concept, or publication-level novelty need a broader citation and expert review.

For an analytical witness certificate, Hu-Kirkland Theorem 3.1.2 can be applied to `e` in both `G` and `G+f`; a standard exact Kemeny formula can separately establish that `f` has zero change. This is a one-instance validation shortcut and does not replace the census proof of minimality.
