## Primary comparisons

**P1, Palacios, Gomez and Del Rio (2014), Hitting Times of Walks on Graphs through Voltages.** Official PDF https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1155/2014/852481; raw-04, source turn502view4, PDF page 2 lines 113-139, Theorem 2 equations (6)-(8); raw-05, turn505view4, pages 3-4 lines 277-384, Corollaries 8-10. The weighted voltage identity is an exact prior ingredient. The cutpoint decomposition is stated for simple random walk. Theorem 2 itself is described by its authors as immediate from Doyle-Snell ingredients. Our inference: apply the weighted identity after increasing one conductance, divide by its strength and pass to the shorting limit. The two endpoint degree increments survive; positivity is target-avoiding voltage support. This is substantial derivational overlap, not merely similar vocabulary. The source does not explicitly state our fork classification in the inspected passages. A weighted extension of its simple-walk corollaries must not be silently attributed to it.

**P2, Markowsky and Palacios (2021), Several consequences of adding or removing an edge from an electric network.** Author preprint https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09706; raw-03, turn500view0, PDF pages 6-7 lines 197-278. Theorem 3 gives the resistance after deleting a non-cut edge as R/(1-R); Theorem 4 gives the corresponding endpoint hitting time for walk-regular graphs. These are exact special perturbation statements with narrower graph/intervention assumptions. They are not an equivalent formulation of uncertain-workload continuous edge-strength regret, or a fork/nonattainment theorem. The perturbation vocabulary itself is plainly prior. The 2021 date is source-dated, not an inferred current publication status.

## Claim-by-claim novelty triage

1. Exact one-edge quadratic-over-linear coefficients: routine rank-one inverse update plus degree increment; retain as implementation mathematics, reject as standalone novel contribution. This judgment follows the displayed derivation already in the paper, supported by P1's foundational identity; it is not an assertion that P1 prints the same coefficient tuple.
2. Worst workload at interval endpoints: routine positive-affine ratio argument. For fixed actions x,y, derivative of Ux/Uy has constant sign; Ux/inf Uy is sup_y Ux/Uy; suprema commute. This is valid without attained oracles. Reject standalone novelty.
3. Unique per-edge optimum and quadratic balance candidate enumeration: constant-sign second derivative plus positive asymptotic slope gives strict quasiconvexity; a maximum of finitely many branches stays strictly quasiconvex; an interior optimum is stationary or a tie. Once the ray formula is known, this is elementary calculus. Exact comparison software is useful but packaging is not the requested novelty.
4. Target-avoiding growth support: substantial known voltage/shorting ingredients, plus justified limit. Classify as likely routine corollary; do not claim an exact prior statement without one.
5. Fork-path zero-growth structure: residual precise statement is: for a connected positive weighted graph, missing pair i,j and fixed source a, all target-averaged growth vanishes under a positive uniform target floor iff the contracted underlying graph is a path with endpoints a and the merged pair. Equivalent original graph is a tail from a followed by two leaves i,j. The extra step uses that every internal vertex separates two terminals, forcing a path. This is a short graph argument, not an established nontrivial literature gap. The two-source coercivity corollary then follows because at most one source is exceptional.
6. Exceptional-ray strict decrease and global attainment iff a finite candidate ties the least finite/limit value: finite first-step algebra and infimum bookkeeping after classification. Reject as independently strong novelty absent a sourced nontrivial obstruction it resolves.
