## Investigator algebra, not an attributed theorem

Fix a graph after candidate insertion e, its pseudoinverse P_e, an existing-edge incidence vector b, and a fixed covariance Q>=0. With connected positive-weight graphs and positive Sherman-Morrison denominator, define T_e=tr(Q P_e), r_e=b^T P_e b and s_e=b^T P_e Q P_e b. The known inverse update gives
F_e(delta)=T_e-delta s_e/(1+delta r_e).
For candidates e,f, multiplying F_e-F_f by (1+delta r_e)(1+delta r_f)>0 gives
(T_e-T_f)(1+delta r_e)(1+delta r_f)-delta s_e(1+delta r_f)+delta s_f(1+delta r_e).
This is degree at most two, including linear, constant and identically-zero cases. Root's quadratic observation follows directly; it need not be a novel theorem. Exact switch intervals still require feasible delta bounds, positive denominators, every competing candidate, root ordering and boundary ties. Two pairwise roots do not limit the whole many-candidate envelope to two switches.

For equal-weight single insertions and a common old-edge perturbation, all candidate final volumes agree at each delta. Hence multiplying by the workload volume preserves candidate rankings. It cannot be dropped in comparisons against no action, or if insertion weights differ by candidate. Q must remain fixed; graph-dependent stationary-target Kemeny weights do not automatically satisfy this premise.

Likewise, a sufficient Loewner certificate is elementary: if on1-perp each perturbed candidate satisfies (1-epsilon)L_e <= L_e' <= (1+epsilon)L_e,0<=epsilon<1, inverse order yields T_e/(1+epsilon) <= T_e' <= T_e/(1-epsilon). A unique nominal winner e stays strictly ahead of f if T_e/(1-epsilon)<T_f/(1+epsilon), equivalently epsilon<(T_f-T_e)/(T_f+T_e) for positive traces. This is a sufficient, generally conservative gap certificate; it is not an exact uncertainty radius or proof that tied optimal edges remain tied. No claim of new Loewner methodology is warranted. These algebraic inferences require independent verification before publication.
